## Billing-Payments Domain — The Reconciliation Neighbor

Their rules are **stricter** because of PCI-DSS Level 1:

| Technology | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **IBM MQ** | **Required** for all payment transaction messaging. Kafka explicitly **not approved** for payment-critical paths |
| **Guardium** | **Required** DB monitoring for PCI-DSS scope |
| **PCI-hardened base images** | Required in Helm charts |
| Cardholder data | **Must be tokenized**, never logged/cached in plaintext |
| Double-entry accounting | Required for anything tagged `ledger` |

### Key Exception (Directly Relevant to You)
> **"Reconciliation batch jobs: Python-based reconciliation jobs that do not handle cardholder data may use relaxed messaging requirements (AMQ Streams instead of IBM MQ) with approval."**

## ⚠️ The Governance Tension

Your service sits at a boundary. Here's what matters:

**If your service lives in the claims domain** (reconciling outbound settlement disbursements):
- You follow **claims governance** → Quarkus, Kafka, Instana, ACS
- You're reconciling bank *confirmations* of payments you sent, not cardholder data
- IBM MQ and PCI-DSS likely **don't apply** because you're matching disbursement records, not processing card payments

**But** — the existing `payment-reconciliation-service` in billing-payments uses IBM MQ and Python for its reconciliation batch jobs. If your service needs to consume data from or interact with the billing-payments domain (e.g., cross-referencing premium refunds against claim settlements), you'd need to go through their **published APIs** — direct DB access across domains is forbidden in both handbooks.

**Recommended stack for your service:**

| Choice | Rationale |
|---|---|
| **Java + Quarkus** | Claims domain mandate. No exception needed |
| **Kafka** | Claims events (settlement approved, payment confirmed). Not payment-critical path, so IBM MQ not required |
| **REST + OpenAPI 3.0.3** | Both domains require it |
| **PostgreSQL** | Claims domain standard (Db2 also approved). Needs no special approval |
| **Instana** | Required. Must hit p99 < 500ms |

`★ Insight ─────────────────────────────────────`
**IBM MQ vs Kafka is the sharpest decision here.** Billing-payments mandates IBM MQ for payment flows because of its exactly-once delivery guarantee — critical when you're *moving money*. But your service isn't moving money; it's *verifying that money moved correctly*. That's a read-after-the-fact reconciliation, not a transactional payment path. Kafka's at-least-once semantics are fine for reconciliation events (duplicate confirmations are idempotent to match). If you later need to trigger re-disbursements from unmatched records, that's when you'd need IBM MQ — and that's when you'd need claims-engineering lead + platform-engineering approval.

**Talk to billing-payments-engineering about their reconciliation patterns.** Their existing `payment-reconciliation-service` got a Python exception specifically for batch reconciliation. Even though you'll use Java (claims rules), their file-parsing and exception-queuing patterns are directly reusable.
