### Cross-Domain Conflicts

| Conflict | Claims Domain Says | Billing-Payments Domain Says | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kafka vs IBM MQ** | Kafka is **Preferred** for all async domain events | IBM MQ is **Required** for payment transaction messaging. Kafka is "not approved for payment-critical paths due to at-least-once semantics" | **Use Kafka.** Your service reconciles *after the fact* — it's not on the payment-critical path. Billing-payments' IBM MQ mandate applies to transactional payment flows (money movement), not to confirmation matching. If your service later needs to *trigger re-disbursements*, that specific flow would need IBM MQ and approval from claims-engineering lead + platform-engineering |
| **Python allowance** | Python allowed only for "analytical or ML components" | Python "restricted to reconciliation batch jobs" — the existing `payment-reconciliation-service` runs Python batch recon | **Use Java.** The Python exception in billing-payments was granted specifically for their domain. Claims governance has no such exception for reconciliation workloads. You'd need a formal deviation (ADR + tech lead + platform approval) to use Python, and the justification would be weak since Quarkus handles batch processing fine |
| **PCI-DSS scope** | Not mentioned for claims-payment-system | PCI-DSS Level 1 mandatory for `payment-processing-system` components. Guardium required. PCI-hardened base images required | **Not in PCI scope.** Your service processes bank confirmation files (account numbers, sort codes, amounts) — not cardholder data (PAN, CVV, expiry). However, if bank confirmations contain card-last-four or tokenized PAN for card-paid claims, consult `parasol-security-engineering` to confirm scope |
| **Database monitoring** | No Guardium requirement | Guardium required for all datastores in PCI-DSS scope | **Guardium not required** — follows from PCI scope resolution above. Standard Instana DB monitoring is sufficient |
| **Data residency** | No explicit requirement | "Storing any data outside the EU" requires approval | **Confirm with claims-engineering.** Bank confirmation files likely contain EU personal data (claimant names, IBANs). Even though claims governance doesn't explicitly mandate EU residency, GDPR registration is mandatory for PII-processing services. Ensure your PostgreSQL instance is EU-region |
| **Double-entry accounting** | Not required | Required for components tagged `ledger` | **Not required.** Your service is a reconciliation engine, not a ledger. The `billing-account-ledger-service` is the ledger. You match records and produce events — you don't maintain financial balances |
| **Direct DB access** | "Direct database access from services outside the claims domain" is **Forbidden** | Same principle — cross-domain access via published APIs only | **No conflict.** You access billing-payments data (if needed) via `policy-query-api` or billing APIs. Within the claims domain, direct service-to-service REST calls are standard |

`★ Insight ─────────────────────────────────────`
**The catalog did the heavy lifting here.** Every technology choice traces back to either a TechDocs handbook (`domain:default/claims`, `domain:default/billing-payments`) or a catalog entity relationship (`claims-payment-system` component graph). Without the catalog, you'd be making these decisions in a vacuum or learning about IBM MQ vs Kafka conflicts after you've already built the wrong thing.

**The cross-domain conflicts are the real value of this analysis.** A developer looking only at the claims handbook would never know that the billing-payments domain mandates IBM MQ for payment flows. The conflict only surfaces when you look at *both* handbooks side-by-side and realize your service sits at the boundary. This is exactly the kind of architectural landmine that a software catalog is designed to expose before it becomes a production incident.

**Three of the four "human decision" items are about scope boundaries, not technology.** The technology choices are clear from governance. What's ambiguous is *which governance applies* — and that's a people decision, not a code decision. Get those clarified before writing a line of code.
